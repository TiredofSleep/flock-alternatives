The Two Jasons

Walking into a room where one owns Flock and believes in it, and the other owns nothing yet. Different pitch for each. Don't blur them.

01 Who's across the table

They care about different things than you do, and about different things from each other. Read each one before you talk.

Committed to Flock
Chief "Shorty" Jackson
Arkadelphia PD
  • Bought Flock in 2024 on a $30,750 Ross Foundation grant — install + 2 free years.
  • On record this Aug: cameras are "another tool in the toolbox" and "here to stay," even as 6+ AR cities pulled out.
  • Honest tell: he's described a Flock search of his own that came up fruitless. He's a realist, not a zealot.
  • Role for you: reference + reality check. Not your buyer. Do not make him defend his 2024 call.
Greenfield
Sheriff Jason
Clark County — no system yet
  • County runs nothing so far. Nothing to rip out, nobody's judgment to challenge.
  • His whole job is jurisdiction and budget. Both of your best arguments live there.
  • Role for you: this is your actual customer. Aim the ownership pitch at him.
Timing wedge  2024 + two free years means the grant is running out about now. The first real renewal invoice — the $2.5–3k/camera/year Flock bill the grant was hiding — is landing or about to. That's what turns "own vs. rent" from philosophy into a budget line. You're not interrupting a happy marriage; you're showing up with the invoice.

02 Pick one ask before you walk in

The fastest way to waste this meeting is to bring a philosophy instead of a request. Choose your lead ask; the rest become "later."

Recommended lead

"Let me stand up a small county pilot you'd own." Lowest risk, aimed at the sheriff, needs nobody to cancel anything. A thing that exists out-argues a thing you're proposing.

Second

"Before the county buys into Flock, look at owning instead." Uses the chief's live system as the worked example of recurring cost.

The big one — only if the room is warm

"I want to build and support this for you." That's a real contract and a real 2am-maintenance commitment. Highest bar. Don't lead here.

Your real edge  You're not an outside vendor from Atlanta. You're a local business owner who writes software and lives in the county. That's standing no Flock rep has. Lean on it — it's the whole reason you get the meeting.

03 Translate your three principles into their language

What moves you is not what moves them. Say the version that lands.

Sovereignty → budget control

You hear

"Local and sovereign, nobody owns our data but us."

They hear

"You own the hardware, the software, and the evidence. No vendor raises your price at renewal or holds your data hostage."

No external sharing → liability protection

You hear

"Not being watched by the feds."

They hear

"No outside agency is searching your cameras and putting your name on something you never authorized. You control access and you've got the log to prove it."

Open source → no lock-in

You hear

"Open source, auditable, free as in freedom."

They hear

"The next administration can't get surprised by a price hike, and you're not locked to me either. Nobody's stranded."

Careful — AR is not a sanctuary state  The "keep the feds out" motive that drives you may read as a negative to an Arkansas sheriff who thinks federal cooperation is fine. Don't sell "no ICE." Sell "no access you didn't approve, by accident, with no paper trail" — same architecture, sold as control instead of ideology.

04 The objections that decide it

Have these cold. Each one has sunk a pitch like yours.

But Flock tells us when a stolen car from Little Rock rolls through.
Their single most-valued feature, and you're cutting it. Be precise: you keep the NCIC/ACIC hotlist — that's the national stolen/wanted/warrant data, and it was never Flock's. What you lose is other Flock customers' camera reads. Know that line exactly; don't overclaim it.
Who fixes it at 2am when it goes down?
The question that kills every hobbyist pitch. If the honest answer is "me, alone, free," they see the single point of failure instantly. Bring a real answer: a support arrangement, documented handoff, and the fact that open source means they're not stranded if you get hit by a truck. Flock's actual product was never the camera — it was "you don't have to think about any of this."
Is this even legal for us to run ourselves?
Yes, and the 2025 law fits you almost exactly. Act 668: law enforcement can run ALPR, cross-check NCIC/ACIC, 150-day retention cap, can't sell or transfer data, twice-yearly public compliance reports. Your audit log isn't just good design — the law already requires the reporting. You build and own; the department is operator of record and runs the hotlist checks under their ACIC credentials.
Does this stuff actually work?
Cut both ways, honestly. For: Sheridan PD recovered 9 stolen vehicles and 2 missing kids in its first months; your own chief credits Flock with recent burglary and stolen-vehicle solves. Against: misreads have put innocent people at gunpoint on bad "stolen" hits, and critics argue measured crime impact is minimal. Don't oversell efficacy — you're not selling that cameras work, you're selling who should own the ones they decide to run.

05 Numbers to have on your tongue

Say these without hedging. Each is sourced and verifiable — a sheriff will respect that you didn't round.

  • $2.5–3kFlock's price per camera per year, recurring, forever (Costa Mesa, Moorhead, Shaker Heights contracts).
  • $30,750The Ross Foundation grant that hid APD's real cost. Covered install + 2 years. That cover is expiring.
  • 150 daysArkansas retention cap under Act 668 (2025). Private property owners: 60 days.
  • ~$8–12kOne-time hardware for a 3-camera build. Fixed legal/CJIS/software overhead is the same whether 3 cameras or 30 — which is exactly why county scale beats a tiny city pilot on cost.
  • 4,000+Flock lookups tagged "immigration / ICE+ERO / ICE WARRANT" found in Danville, IL audit logs. Bend, OR: feds hit its database 279 times unauthorized. A "nationwide lookup" setting cities had on without knowing. CA is suing over it.
  • $86MThe Victoria Police plate project an Australian dev matched with open-source OpenALPR. The tech is a decade proven — you're second on tech, first on the ownership model. That's the stronger spot.
  • 6+Arkansas cities that pulled their Flock cameras since July 2026. The political winds are already at your back; you don't have to be the one stirring them.

06 Do not say

Any one of these can end it in the first two minutes.

Don't

Trash Flock as a surveillance menace. The chief committed to it in print — you'd be insulting his judgment to his face.

Instead

"A local-owned option worth understanding before the county expands."

Don't

Claim CJIS/ACIC compliance is solved or easy. These people live with that burden; you'll mark yourself as someone who's never touched it.

Instead

"I'm already working the ACIC path and I know it's the hard part."

Don't

Promise the statewide network. It's not yours to deliver and it makes you sound grandiose.

Instead

"I'll build node one so the network can exist. The county owns a founding seat." (Per-peer, opt-in, logged sharing — a query, not a firehose.)

Don't

Say "I can probably do it for free / on my own." Free reads as unsupported, and unsupported is the 2am problem.

Instead

"Here's what owning it costs, and here's who keeps it alive."

07 Opening and closing lines

Memorize the shape, not the words. Additive, local, concrete.

Open — defuse first"I'm not here to tell you to pull anything out. I build software, I live here, and I want to show you an option for two moments that are coming — the county deciding whether to buy in, and the city's grant running out."

The hook — to the sheriff"The tech isn't experimental. Agencies have run open-source plate reading for a decade. What's missing in Clark County is someone local to own it instead of renting from Atlanta. I'm here. Let me prove it on a small pilot you own outright."

The control line"The difference isn't the camera — it's that when some agency three states over searches your data, you decide whether that's allowed, and you've got the log. A city in California is getting sued right now because Flock had that switch flipped on by default."

Close — make it cheap to say yes"I'm not asking you to commit to a system today. I'm asking to run a small pilot you can see, own, and shut off if you hate it. Come look at the one already running at my shop's lot."

Before the meeting  Stand up the plate cam at the Ozark lot (legal as a property owner, 60-day cap). "It's already reading plates at my shop — come see it" beats any slide you could build. Get it running this weekend.
